# sampled-piano: a recorded piano trio for the 46-second title film

Nobody has heard this file. Everything below was measured or seen in a picture.

## The idea, in five lines

1. A cool-jazz piano trio in D minor at 120 BPM, eighths swung at 63.5 percent, played by recorded instruments: upright bass, brushes and ride, a soft dark piano.
2. Each NO is a cadence that is refused: NO 1 stops on the dominant (A7b9), NO 2 on the flat-two dominant (Eb7). The slab is that same Eb chord, and this time it falls a half step home to Dm6/9; the tag lets the A7b9 resolve too.
3. The picture's own rhythm is played: each time a door lets go the band cuts off and holds its breath for the 0.375 s it falls, and the hit lands out of that silence.
4. Idea 1 struts toward the relative major with a chord every two beats (the busiest bars); idea 2 is a thin two-feel; idea 3 is the plain Dm, Gm7, Em7b5, A7 walked in four, a layer joining at each PASS, and it is the only one that arrives.
5. The four bay notes at the start are the bass alone, D F A D; idea 3 opens on the same D F A, bass alone: the idea nobody pitched is the first thing in the film.

## Performance

Three seeded generators (bass 1959, piano 1957, drums 1961). Bass 4.2 to 11.7 ms behind the beat (mean 8.2), 28 different velocities in 58 notes, nearly legato with a 22 to 38 ms gap. Ride 0.7 to 6.0 ms ahead (mean 3.4). Piano: fingers within 3 ms of each other, 31 of 118 notes more than 5 ms late, never more than 13.9 ms, a pushed chord at most 8 ms late. No drum voice at the same velocity twice running (0 repeats in 225 hits); brush taps alternate two recordings. Bass velocities are trimmed by sample zone and piano velocities by key, because the audition shows 2 to 6 dB level steps between recordings. The three HARD hits are not humanised: each sampled note starts early by its own measured delay (bass 3.7, 2.9, 3.1 ms; piano 0.6 to 3.9 ms; bass drum 3.2; brush 1.5 and 1.8; ride 1.1) plus 0.5 ms.

## Mix chain

- Bass: dsp.saturate 0.55 (reference half its peak), high-pass 36 Hz, low shelf -5.5 dB at 105 Hz, +2 dB at 230 Hz, +5 dB at 900 Hz, low-pass 4.5 kHz; then its own compressor (kit's, 3:1 from 1 dB over the line's RMS, 6 ms attack; up to 4.8 dB, 0.66 dB on average) and its own look-ahead limiter 10.5 dB over the line's RMS (up to 4.3 dB). Centre, room send -27 dB.
- Piano: stereo narrowed to 40 percent of its side (three keys, F#3 G3 Ab3, are out of phase in the preset: correlation -0.23, 4 dB lost in mono; rendered apart and given back 2.5 dB); high-pass 95 Hz, -1.5 dB at 280 Hz, +4.5 dB shelf above 1 kHz. Pan -0.25, send -13 dB.
- Drums, one sampler pass per voice: swirl and taps each with their own look-ahead limiter (up to 10.7 and 8.3 dB off the spike only); ride low-passed at 6.5 and 9 kHz with a 4 dB narrow dip at 3.4 kHz (a partial that showed as a steady line); skip notes darker; hi-hat foot low-passed at 5.2 kHz; bass drum low-passed at 450 Hz. The time-keeping passes are choked 70 ms after each door lets go. Pan +0.06 to +0.30, sends -13 to -24 dB.
- Thuds: fader -16.5 dB, send -17 dB, centre.
- Room: mix.room decay 0.75 s, damp 0.6, predelay 12 ms, one room per stem.
- Stems are brought to -16 LUFS as a sum; room tone added at -68 dBFS less the master's own gain (measured in a first master pass: 0.67 dB); 5 ms fade in and the 300 ms end fade on every stem.
- Master: mix.master, high-pass 30 Hz, no shelf, compressor threshold -16 ratio 1.6 (up to 2.3 dB, 0.35 dB average), -16 LUFS, ceiling -2 dBTP. The limiter takes off up to 4.0 dB and is below unity gain on 4.3 percent of samples.

## Listening guide

1. At 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 s you should hear four bass notes alone, rising D F A D, one as each bay stands up.
2. At 2.5 s one soft piano chord, with brushes already circling underneath.
3. From 4 s the busiest stretch: ride, brushes, the bass climbing to its top note at 6 s, the piano pushing chords ahead of the beat and adding one last jab at 7.5 s.
4. At about 7.6 s everything cuts off; at 8.0 s a single hit (low thud, bass, a short tense piano chord), then nothing until 10 s.

## Known weaknesses

- The hits do not slam, and may be too polite. In the 0.4 s after NO 1 the mix is at -16.0 dBFS RMS, the same as the bar before the break (-16.1); NO 2 is 3.8 dB over its bar and the slab 1.9 dB. They read as events because of the silence before and after, not because they are louder. To make them heavier raise FADER.thud in build.js; the master limiter (already up to 4.0 dB at the hits) will take most of it back unless idea 1 comes down.
- The master limiter is not idle outside the hits: about 2 dB on some peaks in the busiest bars, 4.3 percent of samples in all. The crest factor is 16.8 dB, higher than the first soundtrack's 14.8.
- The bass is held firmly: its own limiter is below unity gain on about 40 percent of its samples. Each zone is one recording with no velocity layers, so a repeated pitch is the same pluck at another level. A third of its energy above 150 Hz comes from EQ and added harmonics whose character is unheard.
- The brush swirl is a 0.6 s one-shot struck on each beat and each swung "and", not a continuous circle; it may read as a row of swishes. This is the least convincing voice. The ride is one recording: every hit is the same waveform at another level.
- The piano is no longer as dark as the preset (the +4.5 dB shelf), and it carries the highest stem peaks (-3.6 dBFS at the winner and slab chords).
- The tilt (6.12) sits toward the dark edge of the window, with 0.38 to spare, while energy above 8 kHz (-32.8 dB) is higher than the first soundtrack's (-35.2), nearly all of it ride and brushes between 8 and 10 kHz. If the cymbals seem bright, lower the low-passes in RIDE_EQ; the tilt will then need more midrange to stay inside.
- HARD hits: the first trace of each is 3.3 to 3.8 ms before the frame and the bulk of the energy 6 to 10 ms after it. Both are inside 10 ms; which one counts as "on the frame" is a judgement.
- The break before the slab is my reading of "builds toward the slab": a swell, then a held breath, then the chord.
- Originality: composed here from common jazz vocabulary (ii-V-i in D minor, a tritone substitute, a three-step rising cell); no tune was quoted or imitated, and nothing checks that except my own intent.

## Decisions the lead may overrule (from the composer's hand-back)

1. The break. Each time a door lets go (7.625, 16.625, 36.625 s, read from video/score.js) the band cuts off and is silent for the 0.375 s the door falls, then the hit lands. I added this because, under a -2 dBTP ceiling at -16 LUFS, the hits could not be made louder than the bar before them without the limiter flattening them. The brief says "the band builds toward the slab": here the build is a brush swell to 36.5 s, then the held breath. To undo it for the slab, in chart.js set BASS bar 19 beat 2 back to length 1 and DRUMS bar 19 roll and swirl back to [1, 3].
2. The piano is brighter than the raw preset: a +4.5 dB shelf above 1 kHz. Without it the mix measured 7.5 dB per octave, outside the brief's tilt window on the dark side.
3. The master has no 8 kHz shelf (every voice is already low-passed) and a lighter compressor (threshold -16, ratio 1.6) than the kit default.
