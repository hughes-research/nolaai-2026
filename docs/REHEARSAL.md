# Rehearsal card

For tonight and for the five minutes before you walk on. The full notes are in `docs/SPEAKER-NOTES.md` and in the presenter view (press S). Figures below are at the deck's default rate of $30 an hour; the slides compute them live.

## The through-line

Your best AI idea is probably wrong, and the boring one is probably worth money. For 90 minutes you hand them a screen, four gates in order (cost, return, effort, potential), where any no ends it, and you make them use it on their own work. Then you put your own project through the same screen in front of them. On minutes alone it fails: $462 a year. You say so and don't flinch, because that honesty is the whole talk. You finish on leadership. The tool hands back minutes. What happens to them is a management decision, and if the time gets wasted that's on the leaders, not the staff. Share the gain.

## Land these word for word

1. "Your best AI idea is probably wrong." (stop, count two) "The boring one you didn't pitch is probably worth thousands."
2. "Any no ends it. That's not the screen failing. That's the screen working."
3. "Use the expensive model once, to write the tool. Then the tool runs for free."
4. "The boring idea on the cash path beats the exciting one off it."
5. "A minute a week is $25 a year."
6. "Don't write these down. This is a made-up building, and its numbers are bigger than ours. The real result is next, and it's smaller."
7. "On minutes alone my own project fails my own screen. $462 a year."
8. "If the freed time gets wasted, in my eyes that's a lack of proper management." Then: "Say no to the exciting. Say yes to the math."

## Running the three exercises

- **Exercise 1 (10 min):** T to start. Walk. At 5:00: "Fewer than six things? Look at question two." At 1:00: "Finish the line you're on."
- **Exercise 2 (5 min):** T to start. At 2:30: "All three exciting? Swap one for a boring one." At 1:00: "Circle your three." Move on when most have three.
- **Exercise 3 (10 min):** T to start. At 5:00: "Skip to Return." Ask "Who has a favorite they suspect is going to die?" First hand near an aisle, right arrow, screen it in column A.
- **Nobody volunteers on 3:** right arrow, click C, ask "How many minutes would one reminder really save?" and read where it stops. At 1:00: left arrow, "Circle the one still standing."
- **Share-out (5 min):** "Who killed their favorite?" One hand: which gate? Then three hands for the boring one. Nobody? "The exciting one died at Effort. Whose died at the same gate?"
